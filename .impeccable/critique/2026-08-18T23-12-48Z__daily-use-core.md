## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Genuinely authored, not generic. The evidence is in the copy and the ranking logic: Home's alert list is ranked by *when waiting starts costing something*, not by data type. `ReadinessSheet`'s "I have this — it's just not in the app yet" names the exact gap between a trunk and a database. The scoring engine (`crm.ts`) was deliberately trimmed to drop checks nobody feeds, rather than faking a green. Counter-evidence: `Calendar.tsx` hard-codes which weekdays are "primary case days" with no explanation — authored for one rep's actual block schedule, silently wrong for anyone else's.

**Deterministic scan:** 3 findings, all `gray-on-color` (contrast quality), zero false positives. ## Overall Impression

The bones are good — this app knows what it is and mostly talks like the person using it, not like a database admin panel. But the newest layer (this session's tag/link/timeline work) landed *on top of* the existing hierarchy rather than *into* it, and two silent-failure gaps sit exactly where the app's core promise is most tested: on Home, which exists to answer "is anything wrong," a dropped connection is invisible; and in per-record notes, a failed save disappears without a trace. Neither is hard to fix. The bigger structural question is navigation — three overlapping ways to reach "is this case ready" is a symptom of feature growth outpacing information architecture, not of doing too much.

## What's Working

1. **The priority ledger on Home.** One ranked "do these first" list, weighted by urgency, instead of five separate alert blocks in source order pretending to be a hierarchy.
2. **Honest-by-construction scoring.** The readiness engine refuses to show green on data it doesn't actually have — checks that nobody feeds got deleted rather than faked.
3. **Consequence copy at the point of action.** Buttons and confirmations explain what a tap will actually do to the record, in the rep's own terms, right where the tap happens.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] A dropped connection looks identical to a genuinely clear day.**
Why it matters: Home's whole job is answering "is anything wrong right now," used exactly in the hospital-basement, bad-signal conditions where a fetch is most likely to fail silently. The rep can walk into a case believing it's covered when the app simply never heard back.
Fix: catch the failed fetch, show a persistent "couldn't reach the server — this may be stale" banner with a last-synced timestamp, and never render the all-clear state on stale data.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] The readiness sheet buries the verdict under this session's new widgets.**
Why it matters: tags and the link picker now render *above* the readiness verdict and shortage checklist, and "Log case" sits as one of three equal-weight buttons rather than the one action that matters. One-handed, mid-corridor, the rep has to scroll past graph-admin controls to reach the thing they opened the sheet for.
Fix: reorder to header → verdict → checklist → one primary "Log case" action; move tags/links/timeline behind a "Details" disclosure.
Suggested command: `/impeccable layout`

**[P1] Counts and knowledge split apart on Inventory itself — the app's own stated thesis.**
Why it matters: PRODUCT.md's core claim is that counts and knowledge live on the same record. On Inventory, tapping an item opens Move; reaching that item's notes takes two more taps through an edit screen. The highest-traffic inventory surface contradicts the product's own premise.
Fix: make the row tap open one item detail view — counts, lot, expiry, notes, timeline together — with Move as an action inside it, not the destination.
Suggested command: `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] Contrast: three real gray-on-color hits, confirmed by the detector.**
Why it matters: `NotesSection.tsx:225` (delete confirmation text), `DaySheet.tsx:219`, `EntityLinkPicker.tsx:182` all render lighter gray text on a saturated background — legible but washed out, on interactive controls.
Fix: swap to white/near-white text on those three saturated backgrounds.
Suggested command: `/impeccable audit`

**[P2] Notes fail silently.**
Why it matters: add/delete/pin/follow-up/promote in the per-record notes block all use `try/finally` with no `catch` — a rejected write just clears the draft with no error shown, and the note is gone from view without ever having saved.
Fix: catch the rejection, keep the draft text, surface an inline error the way DaySheet already does correctly.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (distracted, one-handed, on the move)** — the closest match to how this app is actually used:
- Calendar requires an unlabeled double-tap to open a day (first tap selects, second opens) — interrupted mid-gesture, a wrong second tap lands in the wrong day's full sheet with no memory of why.
- The readiness sheet's entire backdrop closes it on tap — a thumb brushing the sliver of screen above the sheet loses your place in a nine-section scroll.
- A note's delete `✕` sits inches from `edit` and `pin` in a row of small underlined links — a prime one-handed delete-by-accident spot.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent):**
- Readiness status is carried by emoji and color alone (✅/⚠️/❌, colored dots on the calendar) with no text equivalent in several spots.
- The new note-action links (edit/pin/delete/promote) explicitly opt out of the app's own 44px tap-target rule via `min-h-0` — the one screen-wide accessibility guarantee is disabled on the most-used control cluster in Notes.
- No `aria-live` region anywhere in the four core screens — the "Do these first" list appearing after load is announced to no one using a screen reader.

## Minor Observations

- Raw ISO dates ("Expires 2026-09-02") print in a couple of Inventory rows where `formatDateShort` is used everywhere else.
- The link picker's five relation labels (`related / used_in / located_at / part_of / assigned_to`) are graph vocabulary, not rep vocabulary — worth a plainer set of words.
- Home's "Do these first" list has no cap — a bad week could scroll 20 rows deep, which undercuts its own purpose as a triage list.
- `EntityLinkPicker` uses a plain `<select>` for target records with no search — fine today, won't hold up once a list (like catalog items) gets long.

## Questions to Consider

- If Home's entire job is "is anything wrong right now," why does it have no way to say when it last actually knew?
- Are the new tag/link/timeline widgets something a rep asked for, or the database schema surfacing itself onto the screen? What would it look like to earn their place with rep-specific labels instead of generic ones?
- The calendar defaults to a rep filter and shows teammate initials/assignee chips — for a product built for one rep, what is that chrome costing the primary user if the second seat isn't really active yet?
